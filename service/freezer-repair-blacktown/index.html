Chest Freezer Repair in Blacktown

Chest freezers are the workhorses of Blacktown garages, sheds and back verandahs. They are built to last, but they sit in hot spots, take a beating from the weather, and often hold a whole season of meat, bread and meals. Our chest freezer repair covers:

  • Not freezing, or not freezing hard enough. Often a dirty condenser, a thermostat set wrongly or failing, or a worn lid seal.
  • Frost and ice build-up. Usually a lid seal that no longer closes properly, or a lid opened a lot in humid weather.
  • Running all the time. Poor airflow around the freezer, a dirty condenser, a tired seal or an overloaded freezer.
  • Won't start, or clicks and stops. Power supply, start relay, overload or a compressor fault.
  • Water or ice around the base. Defrost water that cannot drain, or a blocked drain plug.
  • Noisy chest freezer. A loose compressor mount, a fan hitting ice or a worn motor.

We repair chest freezers from most brands, in a range of sizes, and we can tell you straight whether a repair is worth it on an older unit. Looking for chest freezer repair near me? We are in Blacktown, and we also repair chest freezers across Sydney's west and north-west.

Upright Freezer Repair

Upright freezers look like a fridge with drawers, and they fail in different ways to a chest. Because they have a fan and a defrost system, the common faults are:

  • Fan failure. The freezer cools unevenly, or the top is warm while the bottom is cold.
  • Defrost heater, timer or sensor failure. Ice builds up on the back wall and blocks the air, and the freezer slowly warms up.
  • Door seal problems. Doors that do not close fully let in warm air and frost.
  • Control board or thermostat fault. The freezer runs too cold, too warm or swings.
  • Blocked drain. Ice or water at the bottom of the freezer.
  • Compressor and sealed-system faults. Less common, but more costly. We will tell you honestly before we go ahead.

What Temperature Should a Freezer Be?

A home freezer should sit at -18°C or colder. At that temperature food stays frozen hard and keeps its quality for months. If your freezer is running warmer than about -15°C, or food feels soft, it is time to look at it.

A cheap freezer thermometer, kept in the middle of the freezer, is the easiest way to know. Don't rely on the dial. The numbers on the dial are only a guide.

My Freezer Has Stopped Working: What Do I Do First?

1

Keep the lid or door shut

A full freezer that stays closed will usually keep food frozen for roughly a day or two. A half-full one will not last as long.

2

Check the power

Is it plugged in, is the switch on, has the safety switch tripped, has the plug been knocked loose? Try a different power point if you can.

3

Check the thermostat dial

It may have been knocked.

4

Look for obvious causes

Is the lid sitting properly? Is something blocking the seal? Is the back of the freezer against a wall with no room to breathe?

5

Don't keep switching it on and off

Repeated restarts can damage the compressor.

6

Move food if it is warming

Put it in another freezer, or into an esky with ice, and keep the most valuable food together. Then book us. The sooner a technician looks at it, the less you lose.

Can I Refreeze Food That Has Thawed?

As a general food safety guide, food that still has ice crystals or is still cold, at 5°C or below, can usually be refrozen, though the quality may drop. Food that has been above 5°C for more than 4 hours should be thrown out. Raw meat, seafood, cooked meals and dairy are the highest risk.

When in doubt, throw it out. For official advice, see the NSW Food Authority.

Should I Repair or Replace My Freezer?

Most freezer faults are worth repairing. A new fan, seal, thermostat, defrost part or relay is usually a small fraction of the cost of a new freezer. Replacement starts to make sense when:

  • The freezer is older than about 12 to 15 years and has a major fault such as a failed compressor.
  • The sealed system is leaking and the unit is old.
  • The cabinet is rusted through, damaged or the foam insulation is wet.
  • The repair quote is more than around half the price of a comparable new freezer.

We will tell you honestly if a repair is not worth it, and the $69 call-out fee covers the visit and the diagnosis.

How to Keep a Freezer Running Longer

Leave some room

Leave a gap of at least 5 to 10 cm around a chest freezer so air can move.

Keep it cool

Keep it out of direct sun and away from heat sources. A garage in a Western Sydney summer is tough on any freezer.

Clean the condenser

Brush or vacuum the condenser once or twice a year.

Check the seal

If the lid or door seal no longer grips a sheet of paper, it needs replacing.

Defrost when needed

Defrost a chest freezer when ice reaches about 5 mm thick.

Don't overpack it

Don't pack it so tightly that cold air cannot move. But keep it reasonably full, because a full freezer holds its cold better than an empty one.

Can you repair a chest freezer?

Yes. We repair chest freezers of most brands and sizes, including units that are not freezing, running constantly, icing up, making noise or tripping the power.

Why is my chest freezer not freezing?

The usual causes are a dirty condenser, a worn lid seal, a faulty thermostat or a compressor fault. Check that it is plugged in, the thermostat is set correctly, and the lid closes tightly. If it still will not freeze, contact us.

Why is my upright freezer icing up?

Usually a door seal that is not closing, or a defrost heater, timer or sensor that has failed. The ice blocks the airflow and the freezer slowly warms up.

How long will food stay frozen if my freezer stops working?

A full freezer that stays closed will usually hold for roughly a day or two. A half-full one will not last as long. Keep the lid or door shut and contact us.

Is it worth repairing an old freezer?

Often yes, if the fault is a seal, fan, thermostat or relay. If the freezer is very old and the compressor or sealed system has failed, replacement may make more sense. We will give you an honest recommendation.
