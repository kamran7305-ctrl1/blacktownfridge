Is Your Freezer Faulty? Common Warning Signs

Freezers usually give notice before they fail completely. Get in touch if you notice any of these:

Food Isn't Frozen Solid

Ice cream is soft, or items are partly thawed.

Temperature Above −15°C

A healthy freezer holds around −18°C.

Heavy Frost or Ice Build-Up

On the walls, lid or shelves.

Compressor Runs Non-Stop

But the freezer never gets properly cold.

Loud Noises

Buzzing, clicking or rattling from the motor or fan.

Water Pooling

Under or around the freezer.

Lid or Door Won't Seal

The gasket is torn or loose.

Warm to Touch or Burning Smell

The freezer is warm on the sides.

Rising Power Bills

Creeping up for no clear reason.

Why Is My Freezer Not Freezing?

Here are the causes we see most often, from simplest to most serious:

  1. Temperature dial set wrongly. Someone may have bumped it. Check it first.
  2. Blocked airflow or overpacking. Food pressed against vents or the back wall stops cold air circulating.
  3. Worn door or lid seal. Warm air leaks in, and frost builds up around the edge.
  4. Dirty condenser coils. Dust makes the freezer run hotter and work harder.
  5. Failed fan motor. Cold air isn't moved through the cabinet.
  6. Defrost system fault. Ice blocks the evaporator coil and air flow drops.
  7. Faulty thermostat or sensor. The freezer stops cooling at the wrong temperature.
  8. Compressor or start relay failure. The motor hums, clicks or won't run.
  9. Refrigerant leak. The freezer runs but never gets cold enough.

Causes 1 to 4 are quick checks you can do yourself. For anything else, please don't dismantle the freezer. Refrigerant work needs a licensed technician, and electrical faults can be dangerous.

Quick Checks Before You Call

These simple steps can save you a service fee, or at least help us diagnose faster:

  • Make sure the freezer is plugged in and the power point works
  • Check the circuit breaker hasn't tripped
  • Look at the temperature setting
  • Clear any food blocking vents
  • Gently wipe the door or lid seal and check it closes on a sheet of paper
  • Note any noises, error codes or when the fault began
  • Leave the lid or door closed to protect your food

What To Do With Your Food If a Freezer Fails

Keep the lid or door shut. As a general guide, a full freezer can stay frozen for roughly 48 hours without power, and a half-full one for about 24 hours. Packed freezers stay colder longer, so group items together. Move high-value items to an esky with ice or a neighbour's freezer if you can.

Food that still has ice crystals or feels as cold as if refrigerated can usually be refrozen. Food that has thawed and been above 5°C for over four hours should be thrown out, particularly meat, seafood and dairy. When in doubt, throw it out. For official advice, see the NSW Food Authority.

Is It Worth Repairing a Freezer?

Often, yes. Faults like seals, thermostats, fans and defrost parts are usually inexpensive compared with the cost of a new freezer. As a general rule:

  • Repair if the freezer is under about 8 to 10 years old and the repair costs less than half the price of a new one.
  • Consider replacing if it's very old, the compressor has failed, there's rust or damage, or it has broken down repeatedly.
  • Check the running costs. Older freezers can use much more electricity than modern, energy-rated models.

We'll give you honest advice on the spot. If a repair isn't worth it, we'll say so.

Keep Your Freezer Healthy

  • Keep it at −18°C
  • Don't overfill; leave room for air to move
  • Defrost a chest freezer when ice is about 5mm thick
  • Clean the condenser coils once or twice a year
  • Check seals regularly
  • Keep it in a cool, ventilated spot, not in direct sun or a hot garage
  • Don't open the lid more than you need to
  • Fix small noises or frost early

Is it worth repairing an old freezer?

If the freezer is under about 8 to 10 years old and the repair is less than half the price of a new one, usually yes. We'll give you honest advice.

Why is my freezer running but not freezing?

Common causes are dirty coils, a failed fan, a weak seal, a defrost fault, a faulty thermostat or a refrigerant leak. A technician can test and confirm.

Why is there so much ice in my freezer?

Heavy frost usually points to a worn seal, a defrost fault or a door left ajar. Frost-free freezers that ice up often have a faulty defrost heater, timer or sensor.

How long does a freezer last?

Many freezers last 10 to 20 years if well maintained, though budget models and heavily used units may wear out sooner.

Is it safe to refreeze thawed food?

Food with ice crystals that is still cold can usually be refrozen. Food that has been above 5°C for more than four hours should be thrown out.
