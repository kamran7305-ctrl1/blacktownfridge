Why Is My Fridge Not Cooling?

These are the causes we find most often, from the simplest to the most serious:

  1. Blocked vents or an overpacked fridge. Food pressed against the back wall or the air vents stops cold air circulating.
  2. Dirty condenser coils. Dust and pet hair stop the fridge shedding heat, so it runs hot and warm.
  3. Worn door seal. Warm air leaks in and the fridge struggles to keep up.
  4. Failed evaporator fan. The fan that moves cold air from the freezer into the fridge has stopped.
  5. Failed condenser fan. The compressor overheats because heat is not being pulled away.
  6. Defrost system fault. A bad heater, timer or sensor lets ice build up on the evaporator and block airflow.
  7. Faulty thermostat or temperature sensor. The fridge reads the wrong temperature and stops cooling too early.
  8. Control board failure. The board no longer tells the compressor or fans to run.
  9. Start relay or compressor failure. The compressor hums, clicks or won't start.
  10. Refrigerant leak. The fridge runs but never gets properly cold.

Causes 1 to 3 are quick checks you can do yourself. For anything else, please don't take the fridge apart. Sealed-system work and live electrical parts need a trained, licensed technician.

Fridge Not Working Repair: Symptom by Symptom

Fridge Not Cold Enough

A fridge should hold between 3°C and 5°C. If a thermometer on the middle shelf reads above 5°C, food is already at risk. This is the early stage of most cooling faults. A dirty condenser, a tired door seal, a weak fan or a drifting thermostat can all leave the fridge sitting a few degrees too warm, long before it stops altogether. Fixing it at this stage is usually the cheapest repair.

Fridge Light On but Not Cooling

The light only proves the fridge has power. It says nothing about the cooling system. Stand next to the fridge in a quiet room and listen. If you hear no compressor at all, the start relay, overload, control board or compressor itself may have failed. If the compressor runs but the fridge stays warm, the usual suspects are a stopped condenser or evaporator fan, a very dirty condenser, a blocked air path or a refrigerant leak.

Fridge Warm but Freezer Cold

This is the fault we are called to most. In a frost-free fridge, one evaporator sits in the freezer and a fan pushes some of that cold air through a vent into the fridge. When the fan fails, the vent ices over or the defrost system stops working, the freezer carries on freezing while the fridge slowly warms. Pushing everything away from the back wall sometimes helps for a day, but it does not fix the cause. A technician can clear the ice, find out why it formed and replace the part that failed.

Fridge Fan Not Working and Fan Motor Replacement

There are normally two fans. The evaporator fan sits behind the back panel and moves cold air around the cabinet. The condenser fan cools the compressor at the back or the bottom. Signs of a failed fan are a warm fridge section, uneven cooling, a fan that squeals, rattles or runs slowly, or a compressor that is hot and running constantly. Fridge fan motor replacement is one of the most common mid-range repairs. We test the motor and the wiring first, so you are not paying for a new motor when the real problem is ice or a sensor.

Fridge Beeping

A beeping fridge is nearly always sounding an alarm. The most common reasons are a door that has not fully closed, a fridge or freezer running above its safe temperature, or a power interruption that has been logged. Close the doors firmly, check the display and see if the alarm clears. If it keeps beeping with the doors shut, or shows an error code, the cause is usually a temperature sensor, a fan or the control board. Don't just mute it, because the alarm is often telling you the cooling has already failed.

Fridge Running Constantly

A fridge is meant to cycle on and off. If the compressor runs almost all the time, the fridge is fighting to reach its set temperature. Dirty coils, a worn door seal, a failing fan, a door that does not close fully, poor ventilation around a built-in unit or a slow refrigerant leak are the usual causes. It wears the compressor and adds to your power bill, so it is best to deal with it before it turns into a not-cooling fridge.

Fridge Not Working at All

If the fridge is completely silent with a dead display, start with the power point and the safety switch. If those are fine, the cause may be a power supply fault, a blown thermal fuse or a failed control board. If the fridge clicks every few minutes but never runs, the start relay or compressor is the likely fault.

Fridge Control Board Repair

The control board is the fridge's brain. It reads the temperature sensors and switches the compressor, fans and defrost heater on and off. Boards fail from power surges, moisture, heat and age. Symptoms include a dead or flickering display, settings that reset themselves, fans and compressor that do not respond, and error codes. Many faults blamed on the board are really a cheap sensor, a connector or a failed fan the board is reporting, so we test the whole circuit first. Where a board does need replacing, we tell you the price before we start.

Quick Checks Before You Call

These safe, simple steps can save you a service fee, or at least help us diagnose faster:

  • Check the fridge is plugged in and the power point works. Try a lamp in the same socket
  • Check the safety switch or circuit breaker hasn't tripped
  • Look at the temperature setting. Aim for about 3°C to 5°C in the fridge and -18°C in the freezer
  • Make sure the doors are fully closed and nothing is stopping the seal
  • Pull food away from the back wall and clear the air vents
  • Check the door seal grips a sheet of paper
  • Leave 5 cm or more of space around the fridge so it can breathe
  • Listen for the compressor and fans, and note any clicking, humming or error codes

What To Do With Your Food While the Fridge Is Not Cooling

Keep the doors shut. A closed fridge holds its cold for around four hours, and a full freezer for roughly a day or two. Move meat, seafood, dairy, cooked leftovers, baby milk and medication first, into an esky with ice or frozen packs, or into a working freezer.

As a general food safety guide, cold food that has been above 5°C for less than 2 hours can go back in the fridge or be used. Between 2 and 4 hours, use it straight away. After 4 hours, throw it out. When in doubt, throw it out. For official advice, see the NSW Food Authority.

How To Stop Your Fridge Losing Its Cool

  • Clean the condenser coils once or twice a year
  • Don't overfill the shelves or block the air vents
  • Wipe the door seals and check they close properly
  • Leave space around the fridge for air to move
  • Let hot food cool before it goes in
  • Keep the fridge out of direct sun and away from the oven
  • Defrost and clean the drain hole if your model has one
  • Fix small noises, frost and warm spots early

Fridge Not Cooling Repair FAQs

Why is my fridge not cooling but the light is on?

The light only tells you the fridge has power. The cooling system is separate. The usual causes are a failed start relay or compressor, a stopped condenser or evaporator fan, a dirty condenser, a faulty temperature sensor or control board, or a sealed-system leak. Listen for the compressor. If it never starts, or clicks and stops, a technician needs to test it.

Why is my fridge warm but the freezer cold?

In most frost-free fridges, a fan blows cold air from the freezer into the fridge. If the evaporator fan has failed, a vent is blocked with ice, a damper is stuck or the defrost system has iced up the coil, the freezer stays cold while the fridge warms up. It is one of the most common faults we repair, and usually fixable in one visit.

Why is my fridge beeping?

A beeping fridge is nearly always giving you an alarm. The most common reasons are a door left slightly open, the fridge or freezer running too warm, or a power interruption. Close the doors firmly, check the display and see if the beeping stops. If it keeps going, or shows an error code, there may be a sensor, fan or control board fault.

My fridge fan is not working. Can it be repaired?

Yes. A failed evaporator or condenser fan motor can be replaced, and sometimes a fan that is only stuck on ice can be freed by fixing the defrost fault behind it. Fan motor replacement is a mid-range repair and a very common one.

Can you repair a fridge control board?

Often, yes. Control boards and display panels can fail from power surges, moisture or age. We test the board, the sensors and the wiring first, because many faults blamed on the board turn out to be a cheap sensor or connector. If the board has to be replaced, we tell you the price before we start.

Why is my fridge running constantly?

A fridge that never switches off is working too hard. Common causes are dirty condenser coils, a worn door seal, a faulty fan, poor ventilation around the fridge or a refrigerant leak. It is worth fixing early, because constant running wears the compressor and pushes up your power bill.

How much does fridge not cooling repair cost?

Our call-out fee starts from $69 and it covers the visit and the diagnosis. The repair price depends on the fault. A seal or drain is a smaller job, a fan motor or sensor sits in the middle, and a compressor or sealed-system leak is the largest. We give you an upfront quote before any work starts. Seniors receive a discount.

Is it worth repairing a fridge that is not cooling?

If the fridge is under about 10 years old and the fault is a fan, sensor, relay, seal or defrost part, repair usually makes sense. If a very old fridge has a failed compressor or a leaking sealed system, replacement is often the better call. We will tell you honestly.

How long can food stay in a fridge that is not cooling?

Cold food that has been above 5°C for less than 2 hours can go back in the fridge or be used. Between 2 and 4 hours, use it straight away. After 4 hours, throw it out. Keep the doors closed, move high-risk food into an esky with ice or into a working freezer, and when in doubt, throw it out.
