Most fridge faults show up as a few clear symptoms. The table shows what usually causes each one and how our fridge mechanics fix it.

Fridge not cooling, freezer still cold Failed evaporator fan motor, blocked air damper or iced-up evaporator coil Replace the fan or damper, clear the ice and repair the defrost system

Safe Checks Before You Call

Make sure the fridge is set to 3–5°C and the freezer to about −18°C. Check the door seal closes on a sheet of paper, and that the vents inside aren't blocked by food. Vacuum any dust off the condenser coils at the back or bottom. If the fridge still isn't cold after 12–24 hours, book a fridge technician.

Food safety tip: Food Standards Australia New Zealand advises keeping cold food at 5°C or below. If your fridge has been above 5°C for more than 4 hours, perishable food should be thrown out.

What Your Fridge's Error Code Means

Many modern fridges report their own faults. Common examples we see in Blacktown:

  • Samsung 22E or 21E: fridge or freezer fan fault, often caused by ice around the fan.
  • Samsung 5E: fridge defrost sensor fault.
  • Samsung "OF OF": demo/cooling-off mode is on, so the fridge won't cool. It isn't a breakdown.
  • LG Er FF: freezer fan fault. LG Er dH: defrost heater fault. LG Er rS: fridge sensor fault.

An error code shows which system is affected, not which part failed. A technician still needs to test before replacing anything.

Should You Repair or Replace Your Fridge?

A well-maintained fridge usually lasts 10 to 15 years. As a rule of thumb, repair it if the fridge is under 10 years old and the repair costs less than half the price of a comparable new fridge. Consider replacing it if it's over 12–15 years old and needs a compressor, or it has a sealed-system fault on an entry-level model. Our technicians will tell you honestly when a repair isn't worth it.

Preventative Maintenance Tips

A little upkeep goes a long way in Blacktown's climate, especially heading into summer.

Residential fridges & freezers

Vacuum the condenser coils a couple of times a year, keep the unit a few centimetres off the wall for airflow, check door seals for cracks and gaps, and don't overpack, air needs to circulate to cool evenly.

Commercial refrigeration

Schedule regular coil cleaning and gasket checks, keep condensers clear of kitchen grease, and log temperatures daily so a slow drift is caught before it becomes a breakdown.

Coolrooms

Inspect door seals and closers, keep evaporators clear of ice, and book periodic servicing, a coolroom failure is far cheaper to prevent than to recover from.

Display fridges

Clean glass, fans and drains regularly, and never block internal vents with stock, restricted airflow is the number one reason display units lose temperature.

Freezers

Defrost before ice gets thick, check the seal, and keep the unit out of direct heat and sun where you can.

Why is my fridge not cooling but the freezer is cold?

It's usually a failed evaporator fan, an iced-up evaporator coil from a defrost fault, or a stuck air damper. The freezer stays cold because it sits next to the cooling coil, but cold air isn't reaching the fridge. A technician can usually fix this in one visit.

Is it worth repairing an old fridge?

It's generally worth it if the fridge is under 10 years old and the repair costs less than half the price of a new one. Fridges over 12–15 years old that need a compressor are usually better replaced.
